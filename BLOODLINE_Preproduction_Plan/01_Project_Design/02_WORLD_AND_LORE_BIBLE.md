# World and Lore Bible

## Status and intent

This is an original-world proposal for a fighting game. Names and lore are provisional and should be revised after the core roster and tone are approved. The setting mixes ancient traditions, present-day life, advanced organisations, and supernatural forces without presenting itself as a real historical setting.

## World concept

The world contains old fortified settlements, ritual sites, industrial zones, dense contemporary cities, private research campuses, and high-tech facilities. A supernatural phenomenon known as **the Veil** has existed in folklore for generations. Most people consider it myth. A small number of people can interact with it, producing unusual abilities that are personal rather than universally identical.

## Design rule: supernatural power has constraints

The Veil should not solve every problem. Powers need costs, limits, cooldowns, ranges, tells, and counterplay. A defensive power may absorb a brief impact but cannot be active indefinitely. A displacement technique covers a defined distance and can be punished if used predictably. A forceful attack may be powerful but leave the user vulnerable. These rules support gameplay balance and story tension.

## Major locations

### Veyra City (working name)
A dense contemporary city containing residential blocks, elevated transit, industrial corridors, entertainment districts, and hidden fight venues. It represents the modern face of the setting.

### The Old Quarter
Older stone structures, courtyards, training grounds, shrines, and narrow lanes. This area preserves combat traditions and local legends.

### The Foundry Belt
Industrial workshops, freight yards, abandoned factories, and equipment makers. It supports heavy, industrial, and weapon-focused character themes.

### The Meridian Institute
A powerful research organisation studying the Veil. Its polished, controlled architecture contrasts with the unstable phenomena it attempts to exploit.

### The Shattered Sanctum
An old site linked to the earliest known Veil events. It can later serve as a campaign location and a visually distinct arena.

These are functional art/story anchors, not promises of an explorable open world. Early arenas can be self-contained locations inspired by them.

## Factions

### The Covenant
A covert organisation seeking to control Veil users and systematise their abilities. It includes researchers, enforcers, political allies, and internal dissenters. Avoid reducing every member to a faceless villain; competing motives create better encounters.

### The Old Guard
Traditional schools, clans, and guardians who believe the Veil must be respected and contained. They are divided over whether secrecy protects society or preserves abuses of power.

### The Free Circuit
An underground network of fighters, promoters, equipment traders, and informants. It creates a natural reason for versus matches and tournaments without requiring the full story campaign.

### Independent Awakened
Individuals with different origins and goals. Some seek justice, some profit, some answers, and others simply control over their lives.

## Character-story rules

Every major fighter should have: a personal goal, a belief about the Veil, a relationship to at least one other fighter or faction, a reason to fight, and a meaningful weakness or contradiction. The roster should contain rivals and allies, not six unrelated costumes.

## Tone

Serious action with moments of personality and relief. Combat can be dramatic and intense, but the world should remain coherent rather than shifting from grounded martial arts to random spectacle without rules. Visual effects should express the individual fighter's power and psychology.

## Visual storytelling

Use costume motifs, scars, gear, stance, and colour accents to imply history without requiring paragraphs of dialogue. Avoid copying iconic costume shapes or effects from existing franchises. Historical and cultural references should be researched and used respectfully; do not mix sacred objects or real traditions carelessly just because they look “mystical.”

## Lore boundaries

- Do not use real countries, ethnic groups, religions, or historical conflicts as simplistic villains.
- Do not assume every fighter needs supernatural powers; a skilled, trained, or technologically equipped fighter can be competitive.
- Avoid unlimited power escalation. New content must obey the established Veil rules.
- Keep names, symbols, silhouettes, lore, music, voice performances, and animation designs original.

## Future campaign hooks

The first campaign may follow one protagonist, with supporting fighters introduced through encounters. Later chapters can investigate the first Veil incident, the Covenant's divisions, the Old Guard's hidden history, and the origin of the Shattered Sanctum. These are future content hooks, not launch dependencies.
