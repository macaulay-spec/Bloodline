# Character Roster Bible

## Roster policy

BLOODLINE uses predefined fighters. Each fighter is a designed, authored character with their own identity and curated moveset. The initial roster should be small enough to complete and balance. Start with one production-standard fighter; do not commission or model a large roster before validating the pipeline.

All names below are working names. Conduct name, trademark, cultural, and visual originality checks before public release.

## Shared character specification

Every fighter record must contain:
- Stable character ID and display name.
- Narrative role, faction, personality, motivation, and key relationships.
- Intended combat archetype, strength, weakness, range, movement speed, and defensive options.
- Base stats and move-set reference.
- Signature ability, resource/cooldown rules, range, startup, active duration, recovery, and counterplay.
- Weapon compatibility and equipment constraints.
- Model, materials, textures, rig, animations, portraits, icons, VFX, and audio references.
- Unlock condition, mastery rewards, localisation keys, asset-license references, and validation status.

## Candidate launch archetypes

### 1. The Unbound — balanced close-range fighter
**Role:** adaptable protagonist-shaped fighter for onboarding.  
**Strength:** accessible tools, reliable combo routes, straightforward special.  
**Weakness:** no extreme range or overwhelming specialist advantage.  
**Signature concept:** temporarily reinforces a defined set of attacks through Veil energy.  
**Design goal:** teach core attack, defense, spacing, and resource use.

### 2. Nyra — agile counter-fighter
**Role:** quick, evasive specialist.  
**Strength:** movement, whiff-punishing, quick follow-ups.  
**Weakness:** lower individual hit damage and a more demanding defensive rhythm.  
**Signature concept:** limited displacement dash with a visible start-up cue and punishable recovery if mistimed.  
**Design goal:** reward timing and positional awareness.

### 3. Dagan — armored defensive powerhouse
**Role:** deliberate heavy fighter.  
**Strength:** guard, heavy strikes, strong punish windows.  
**Weakness:** slower movement and less ability to escape pressure.  
**Signature concept:** brief defensive reinforcement that must be timed and cannot be held indefinitely.  
**Design goal:** teach defense, commitment, and punishing mistakes.

### 4. Kael — pressure fighter
**Role:** aggressive close-range attacker.  
**Strength:** pressure strings and momentum when offense connects.  
**Weakness:** unsafe or predictable attacks can be punished.  
**Signature concept:** consecutive successful attacks build limited heat, enhancing a defined follow-up.  
**Design goal:** create a distinct rhythm without endless combos.

### 5. Sable — technical space controller
**Role:** manages range and interrupts predictable approaches.  
**Strength:** precision, controlled reach, disruption.  
**Weakness:** requires reads and has limited reward when attacks are used carelessly.  
**Signature concept:** a short-lived spatial distortion with strict range and recovery.  
**Design goal:** teach spacing and decision-making.

### 6. Voss — heavy antagonist/boss candidate
**Role:** deliberate, intimidating, high-commitment combatant.  
**Strength:** strong reach, punishing attacks, unique encounter patterns.  
**Weakness:** exposed recovery windows and predictable telegraphs.  
**Signature concept:** an unstable burst that trades safety for power.  
**Design goal:** work as a readable AI challenge, not a cheating opponent.

## Recommended initial roster policy

The vertical slice uses one complete fighter and one opponent. The first release target is three to five fully validated fighters, not all six necessarily. Voss may be held until boss tuning is available. A fighter is not “done” if it only has a model or if it shares every move with another fighter.

## Balance principles

- Every fighter must have at least one reliable answer to common pressure.
- Strong moves require a cost: recovery, resource, limited range, predictable timing, or a clear counter.
- A rarity tier must not automatically equal superior competitive performance.
- Matchups should change how a player approaches combat, not make victory impossible.
- Test with human play sessions rather than relying only on numeric intuition.

## Unlock design

Starter choice should be fair. If assigned randomly, each candidate must be beginner-viable, and players should have a clear future path to unlock or try another fighter. Avoid permanent random lock-in. Unlock criteria must be visible, achievable, and recorded in player progression.

## Naming and originality checklist

Before approval, search for confusingly similar names and registered marks; review silhouettes, costume patterns, weapons, special-move presentations, voice lines, and lore against major fighting franchises. “Inspired by genre conventions” is acceptable; copying recognisable expression is not.

## Character approval gate

Approve a character only after concept sign-off, model review, topology/material review, rig test, animation pass, combat-data pass, VFX/audio check, Android performance check, license check, and playtest/balance review. Record defects and version every iteration.
